1. Wildlife

Wildlife means all plants, animals and other living things that naturally grow or live in natural places without being tamed by humans.

It includes animals, birds, reptiles, insects, plants and fungi. Wildlife is found everywhere, from deep jungles to city parks.

Why wildlife matters

Wildlife is important because it gives us:

  1. Balance – every species helps keep its ecosystem healthy
  2. Resources – food, medicine, clean water and fresh air
  3. Value – beauty, culture, science and learning

Wildlife photography

Wildlife photography is capturing the beauty of wildlife in a still picture.

To freeze the sudden movement of wild animals, you need to set up your camera to prevent blurry images (shutter speed).

Core camera settings for action

To prevent blurry images, photographers rely on a specific setup:

  1. Fast shutter speed
  2. Continuous Autofocus (AF-C)
  3. Burst Mode
  4. Wide Aperture

2. Learn about lifestyle

Every wild animal has its own way of living. Where it sleeps, when it hunts or feeds, and whether it lives alone or in a group is called its lifestyle. Here is how the eight animals in this gallery live.

Three ways of living

  • Group life – lions, wolves and hippos live and work together.
  • Solitary life – tigers, leopards, crocodiles, snakes and most bears live alone.
  • Seasonal life – many bears rest through winter, living off stored body fat.

4. Explore the wildlife

Wildlife refers to undomesticated animals and uncultivated plant species which can exist in their natural habitat, but has come to include all organisms that grow or live wild in an area without being introduced by humans. Wildlife was also synonymous to game, birds and mammals hunted for sport. Wildlife can be found in all ecosystems, both wild and most developed urban areas, forming distinct groups.

Wild words

Habitat
The natural home of an animal or plant.
Predator and prey
A predator hunts other animals; the hunted animal is its prey.
Herbivore, carnivore, omnivore
Eats only plants, mainly meat, or both plants and meat.
Camouflage
Colours or patterns that help an animal blend into its surroundings.
Ecosystem
A community of living things and their environment, all connected together.

6. Animal details

Full details of every animal in the gallery.

The Lion

Panthera leo

Red · Courage

Lions are the most social of all the big cats. They live in family groups called prides. The lionesses do most of the hunting, often working together, while the males defend the territory. The male's thick mane makes him easy to recognise.

HabitatAfrican savannas and grasslands; a small wild population lives in India's Gir Forest
SizeMales about 150–250 kg, females about 120–180 kg
DietCarnivore: zebra, wildebeest, buffalo, antelope
LifespanAbout 10–14 years in the wild
ConservationVulnerable
Fun facts
  • A lion's roar can be heard from about 8 km away.
  • Lions can sleep for up to 20 hours a day.
  • Cubs are born with spots that fade as they grow.

The Tiger

Panthera tigris

Orange · Fire

The tiger is the largest of all the wild cats. Unlike many cats, tigers love water and are strong swimmers. They live alone and patrol large areas of forest, hunting mostly at dusk and during the night. Their stripes help them blend into tall grass and shadows.

HabitatForests, grasslands and mangroves of Asia
SizeAbout 100–300 kg depending on sex and subspecies; up to about 3 m long including the tail
DietCarnivore: deer, wild boar, wild cattle
LifespanAbout 10–15 years in the wild
ConservationEndangered
Fun facts
  • No two tigers have the same stripe pattern.
  • The stripes go all the way into the skin, not just the fur.
  • Tigers are excellent swimmers and often cool off in water.

The Leopard

Panthera pardus

Yellow · Sunlight

Leopards are secretive, powerful and extremely adaptable. They live in everything from rainforests to mountains and even near villages. Their golden coat is covered in spots called rosettes. Leopards are strong climbers and often rest on tree branches by day.

HabitatForests, savannas and mountains across Africa and Asia
SizeAbout 30–90 kg
DietCarnivore: antelope, monkeys, rodents, birds
LifespanAbout 12–17 years in the wild
ConservationVulnerable
Fun facts
  • Leopards can carry heavy prey up into a tree to keep it safe.
  • Each leopard has its own unique pattern of rosettes.
  • Some leopards are born almost black and are known as black panthers.

The Crocodile

Family Crocodylidae

Green · Swamp

Crocodiles are patient ambush hunters. They float almost invisibly with only their eyes and nostrils above water, then strike in a flash. Their relatives have survived on Earth for over 200 million years. Mother crocodiles guard their nests and carry newly hatched babies to the water in their mouths.

HabitatRivers, lakes, swamps and coasts in warm regions
SizeMost species are 2–5 m long; the largest can pass 6 m
DietCarnivore: fish, birds, and mammals that come to drink
LifespanOften 50–70 years
ConservationVaries by species
Fun facts
  • Crocodiles have one of the strongest bites in the animal world.
  • The temperature of the nest can decide whether babies are male or female.
  • They bask in the sun with their mouths open to cool down.

The Hippo

Hippopotamus amphibius

Blue · Water

Hippos spend the day cooling off in rivers and lakes, then come out at night to graze on grass. The name means "river horse", though they are not related to horses. They look calm but are among the most dangerous animals in Africa, especially when defending their territory.

HabitatRivers, lakes and swamps of sub-Saharan Africa
SizeAbout 1,300–1,800 kg, around 1.5 m tall at the shoulder
DietHerbivore: mainly grass, eaten at night
LifespanAbout 40–50 years
ConservationVulnerable
Fun facts
  • Hippos make a reddish oily fluid that helps protect their skin from the sun.
  • They can hold their breath for about five minutes underwater.
  • Their closest living relatives are whales and dolphins.

The Wolf

Canis lupus

Indigo · Twilight

Wolves live and hunt in family packs led by a breeding pair. They communicate with howls, body language and scent, and the whole pack helps raise the pups. Wolves are intelligent, travel long distances and are the wild ancestors of today's pet dogs.

HabitatForests, mountains, tundra and grasslands of the Northern Hemisphere
SizeMost are about 30–50 kg; the largest can reach about 80 kg
DietMostly carnivore: deer, elk, moose and smaller animals
LifespanAbout 6–8 years in the wild
ConservationLeast Concern overall, though some populations are threatened
Fun facts
  • A wolf's howl can travel many kilometres across open land.
  • Wolf pups are born blind and deaf and depend on the pack.
  • Wolves can travel dozens of kilometres in a single day.

The Snake

Suborder Serpentes

Violet · Mystery

Snakes have no legs, no eyelids and no outer ears, yet they sense the world brilliantly through scent, heat and vibrations. There are thousands of species, and only a minority are dangerous to people. Snakes help nature by controlling pests such as rats and mice.

HabitatAlmost everywhere except Antarctica: forests, deserts, grasslands, rivers and oceans
SizeFrom about 10 cm to over 6 m, depending on the species
DietCarnivore: rodents, birds, eggs, frogs, fish; swallowed whole
LifespanRoughly 10–30 years, depending on the species
ConservationVaries by species
Fun facts
  • A snake flicks its tongue to collect scent from the air.
  • Snakes shed their skin as they grow.
  • Their jaws can stretch wide to swallow prey larger than their head.

The Bear

Family Ursidae

Pink · Warmth

Bears are strong, curious and surprisingly gentle when left alone. There are eight species, from small sun bears to giant brown and polar bears. They eat almost anything: berries, roots, insects, fish and meat. In cold regions many bears spend the winter in a deep rest, living off the fat they stored in autumn.

HabitatForests, mountains and Arctic regions in Asia, Europe and the Americas
SizeFrom about 30 kg (sun bear) to over 500 kg (largest brown and polar bears)
DietMostly omnivore: plants, fruit, insects, fish and meat
LifespanAbout 20–30 years
ConservationVaries by species
Fun facts
  • A bear's sense of smell is far better than a dog's.
  • Despite their size, bears can run very fast over short distances.
  • Bears are good swimmers and climbers.

8. Survival

Survival has two sides: how wild animals survive in nature, and how people can help wildlife survive.

How animals survive

Camouflage

Tiger stripes and leopard rosettes break up the animal's outline so it blends into grass, leaves and shadows.

Teamwork

Lions and wolves hunt and raise their young together, which makes the whole group stronger.

Water and patience

Crocodiles ambush from the water, while hippos stay cool in rivers during the heat of the day.

Special senses

Snakes "taste" the air with their tongues, and bears use a powerful sense of smell to find food.

Winter rest

In cold regions many bears rest through winter and live off the fat they stored in autumn.

Threats and protection

Main threats

  • Habitat loss – forests, wetlands and grasslands are cleared for farms and cities.
  • Poaching – illegal hunting and the wildlife trade threaten many species.
  • Climate change – changing weather shifts the food, water and seasons animals depend on.
  • Pollution – plastic and chemicals harm rivers and the animals that live in them.

How we can help

  • Plant trees and protect local green spaces.
  • Reduce plastic and keep rivers and parks clean.
  • Never buy products made from endangered animals.
  • Keep a safe distance from wild animals and never feed them.
  • Share what you learn so more people care about wildlife.
Tip: Good wildlife photography is about patience and respect. Never chase or startle an animal for a photo; the animal's safety always comes first.
